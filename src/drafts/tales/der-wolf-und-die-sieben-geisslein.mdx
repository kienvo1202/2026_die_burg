<Initial>

A mother goat, seven kids, a locked door, and a wolf who keeps coming back in better
disguise. The youngest kid survives by hiding inside a clock, which is why this tale
belongs after lesson 10. Listen for the voice, the foot and the clock.

</Initial>

## Twelve o'clock

Once there was an old goat who had seven young kids and loved them as any mother loves her
children. One day she had to go into the forest to fetch food, so she called all seven
to her and warned them about the wolf. "He'll try to trick you," she said, "but you'll know
him by his rough voice and his black feet."

<Dialogue title="Before she goes" setting="A little house at the edge of the forest, morning">
  <Line who="Mother" de="Kinder, um zwölf bin ich hier. Tschüss!" en="Children, I'll be back at twelve. Bye!" />
  <Line who="The kids" de="Tschüss, Mutter!" en="Bye, Mother!" />
</Dialogue>

She had hardly gone when someone knocked at the door.

<Dialogue title="The first knock" setting="At the door">
  <Line who="The wolf" de="Kinder, hier ist eure Mutter!" en="Children, your mother is here!" />
  <Line who="The kids" de="Nein! Unsere Mutter hat eine schöne Stimme. Du bist der Wolf!" en="No! Our mother has a lovely voice. You're the wolf!" />
</Dialogue>

## Three knocks

The wolf went to a shop, bought a big piece of chalk and ate it, and his voice came out
soft. He knocked again.

<Dialogue title="The second knock" setting="At the door">
  <Line who="The wolf" de="Kinder, hier ist eure Mutter!" en="Children, your mother is here!" />
  <Line who="The kids" de="Die Stimme ist schön … aber der Fuß ist schwarz! Du bist nicht unsere Mutter!" en="The voice is lovely… but the foot is black! You're not our mother!" />
</Dialogue>

He had laid his black paw on the window sill. So he ran to the baker, who smeared dough on
his paw, and to the miller, who dusted it white with flour. The miller didn't want to; the
wolf said he'd eat him if he didn't. Then the wolf knocked a third time and put his paw on
the sill.

<Dialogue title="The third knock" setting="At the door">
  <Line who="The wolf" de="Kinder, hier ist eure Mutter!" en="Children, your mother is here!" />
  <Line who="The kids" de="Die Stimme ist schön. Der Fuß ist weiß … Mutter!" en="The voice is lovely. The foot is white… Mother!" />
</Dialogue>

They opened the door, and in came the wolf. The kids ran to hide: under the table, into the
bed, into the oven, into the kitchen, into the cupboard, under the washbowl. The youngest
jumped into the case of the clock on the wall. The wolf found them one after another and swallowed
them whole, all but one. Then, full and lazy, he lay down under a tree in the meadow and
fell asleep.

## In the clock

<Dialogue title="Twelve o'clock" setting="Inside the clock case">
  <Line who="The youngest" de="Es ist zwölf Uhr. Wo ist unsere Mutter?" en="It's twelve o'clock. Where is our mother?" />
</Dialogue>

The mother came home to an open door, a fallen table and an empty house. The washbowl lay in pieces.

<Dialogue title="The empty house" setting="The little house, noon">
  <Line who="Mother" de="Kinder? Wo seid ihr?" en="Children? Where are you?" />
  <Line who="The youngest" de="Mutter! Ich bin hier!" en="Mother! I'm here!" />
  <Line who="The youngest" de="Der Wolf! Sechs Kinder!" en="The wolf! Six children!" />
</Dialogue>

## Stones

The mother went out with her youngest and found the wolf snoring under the tree. Something
was moving in his belly. She sent the youngest running home for scissors, needle and thread, and as soon as she made
the first cut, a kid put its head out. One after another, all six jumped out, alive: the
wolf had swallowed them whole.

<Dialogue title="Out of the wolf" setting="The meadow">
  <Line who="The kids" de="Hier sind wir, Mutter!" en="Here we are, Mother!" />
</Dialogue>

They filled the wolf's belly with heavy stones and stitched it up again. When the wolf
woke, the stones made him thirsty.

<Dialogue title="The well" setting="The meadow, later">
  <Line who="The wolf" de="Ich habe Durst!" en="I'm thirsty!" />
</Dialogue>

He went to the well and bent over to drink, and the heavy stones pulled him in. He drowned.
The seven kids came running and danced round the well with their mother.

<Dialogue title="Round the well" setting="The well">
  <Line who="The kids" de="Der Wolf ist tot! Der Wolf ist tot!" en="The wolf is dead! The wolf is dead!" />
</Dialogue>

## The real story

**Hear the rules you know.** The wolf says <Say de="eure Mutter" />; the kids answer
<Say de="unsere Mutter" /> (lesson 7). And <Say de="Hier sind wir!" /> keeps the verb
second, with *hier* in first place (lesson 10).

**Geißlein.** <span lang="de">*Geiß*</span> is the southern and western word for a (nanny)
goat, and *-lein* is the old-fashioned, southern-sounding twin of *-chen*: it makes the word little, and a *das* word, just like
*das Brötchen* (lesson 4).

**Wolves don't essen.** German has a separate verb for animals eating,
<span lang="de">*fressen*</span>, so in the Grimms' German the wolf *frisst* the kids. Said of
a person, it's rude: stuffing your face.

**The clock.** The youngest hides in the *Uhrkasten*, the wooden case of the
clock on the wall. The Grimms printed the tale in their first collection in 1812; it is
number 5 of their tales.
