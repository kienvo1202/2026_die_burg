<Initial>

The shortest Grimm tale with the biggest mess. A girl and her mother have nothing to eat,
a magic pot fixes that, and then one wrong moment fills a whole town with porridge. You
know almost every German word in it already, including the *kein* you met in lesson 8.

</Initial>

## Nothing to eat

There was once a poor girl who lived alone with her mother. One day there was nothing left
in the house at all.

<Dialogue title="The empty kitchen" setting="A small house, morning">
  <Line who="Girl" de="Mutter, wir haben kein Brot." en="Mother, we've got no bread." />
  <Line who="Mother" de="Und keine Milch. Und keinen Käse." en="And no milk. And no cheese." />
</Dialogue>

So the girl went into the forest, and there she met an old woman who already knew all
about her trouble. The old woman gave her a little pot.

<Dialogue title="In the forest" setting="A path among the trees">
  <Line who="Old woman" de="Guten Tag! Hier, bitte. Ein Töpfchen." en="Good day! Here you are. A little pot." />
  <Line who="Girl" de="Ein Töpfchen?" en="A little pot?" />
  <Line who="Old woman" de="Töpfchen, koche! … Töpfchen, steh!" en="Little pot, cook! … Little pot, stop!" />
</Dialogue>

Say *Töpfchen, koche!* and it cooks good sweet porridge. Say *Töpfchen, steh!* and it
stops. The girl ran home with it.

<Dialogue title="Home" setting="The little kitchen">
  <Line who="Girl" de="Töpfchen, koche!" en="Little pot, cook!" />
  <Line who="Mother" de="Brei! Er ist süß! Lecker!" en="Porridge! It's sweet! Delicious!" />
  <Line who="Girl" de="Töpfchen, steh!" en="Little pot, stop!" />
</Dialogue>

From then on they were never hungry again.

## The wrong moment

One day, when the girl was out, the mother wanted something to eat. She knew the first
magic word.

<Dialogue title="Alone with the pot" setting="The little kitchen">
  <Line who="Mother" de="Töpfchen, koche!" en="Little pot, cook!" />
</Dialogue>

The pot cooked, and she ate until she was full. Then she wanted it to stop, but she didn't
know the word.

<Dialogue title="The word" setting="The kitchen, filling up">
  <Line who="Mother" de="Töpfchen … nein! Nein! Töpfchen, bitte!" en="Little pot… no! No! Little pot, please!" />
</Dialogue>

The pot cooked on. The porridge ran over the rim, filled the kitchen, filled the house,
went into the next house and out into the street, as if it wanted to feed the whole
world.

<Dialogue title="Outside" setting="The street, a little later">
  <Line who="A neighbour" de="Brei! Die Küche, die Straße, die Stadt!" en="Porridge! The kitchen, the street, the whole town!" />
</Dialogue>

Only one house in the town was still free of porridge when the girl came home and saw it.

<Dialogue title="Just in time" setting="The street, knee-deep">
  <Line who="Girl" de="Töpfchen, steh!" en="Little pot, stop!" />
</Dialogue>

It stopped. And anyone who wanted to get back into town had to eat their way in.

<Dialogue title="The way home" setting="The town gate">
  <Line who="Everyone" de="Wir essen, wir essen, wir essen …" en="We eat, we eat, we eat…" />
</Dialogue>

## The real story

**Hear the rules you know.** *Töpfchen* is <span lang="de">*Topf*</span> plus *-chen*,
so it is a *das* word (lesson 4). And the mother calls the porridge *er*: *der Brei* is a
*der* word (lesson 5).

**The magic words are commands.** <span lang="de">*Koche!*</span> is the "do it!" form of
<span lang="de">*kochen*</span>, and <span lang="de">*steh!*</span> means "stand (still)!".
You'll learn to give commands in a later lesson; for now, they are just magic.

**Not porridge as you know it.** The Grimms' *süßer Brei* is millet porridge, *Hirsebrei*,
a common everyday food long before potatoes arrived.

**Magic you can't stop.** The same problem drives Goethe's poem *Der Zauberlehrling*, *The
Sorcerer's Apprentice* (1797), the one later made famous by Disney's *Fantasia*. The
apprentice starts a broom fetching water and has forgotten the word to stop it.
