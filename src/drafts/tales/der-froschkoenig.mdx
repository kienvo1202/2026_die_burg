<Initial>

A princess drops her golden ball down a well, and a frog offers to fetch it, for a price.
She promises anything, means nothing, and then has to live with what she said. The first
tale in the Grimms' collection runs on *can*, *want* and *must*, which is why it belongs
after lesson 12.

</Initial>

## The well

In olden times, when wishing still helped, there lived a king whose youngest daughter was so
beautiful that the sun itself wondered at her face. Near the castle lay a dark forest,
and in the forest, under an old lime tree, a well. On hot days the princess sat at its
edge and played with her golden ball, throwing it up and catching it. One day it slipped
past her hands, rolled into the well, and was gone.

<Dialogue title="The ball" setting="A well in the forest, a hot afternoon">
  <Line who="Princess" de="Mein Ball! Ich kann nicht schwimmen!" en="My ball! I can't swim!" />
  <Line who="Frog" de="Was hast du, Prinzessin?" en="What's the matter, princess?" />
</Dialogue>

A frog had put his fat, ugly head out of the water.

<Dialogue title="The bargain" setting="At the edge of the well">
  <Line who="Frog" de="Ich kann schwimmen. Aber was bekomme ich?" en="I can swim. But what do I get?" />
  <Line who="Princess" de="Was willst du? Mein Kleid? Meine Schuhe?" en="What do you want? My dress? My shoes?" />
  <Line who="Frog" de="Nein. Ich will dein Freund sein!" en="No. I want to be your friend!" />
</Dialogue>

He wanted more than that, he said: to sit beside her at the table, eat from her little
golden plate, drink from her little cup and sleep in her little bed. The princess thought:
what nonsense this frog talks. He can only sit in the water and croak.

<Dialogue title="The promise" setting="At the edge of the well">
  <Line who="Princess" de="Gut, gut. Du bist mein Freund." en="Fine, fine. You're my friend." />
</Dialogue>

The frog dived, came back up with the ball in his mouth and threw it on the grass. The
princess picked it up and ran off.

<Dialogue title="Left behind" setting="The forest">
  <Line who="Frog" de="Prinzessin! Ich kann nicht laufen!" en="Princess! I can't keep up!" />
</Dialogue>

She didn't listen. By the time she reached the castle she had forgotten all about him.

## At the table

The next evening, as the king and his court sat at dinner, something came climbing up the
marble stairs, *plitsch platsch*, and knocked at the door.

<Dialogue title="A knock" setting="The castle door, evening">
  <Line who="Frog" de="Prinzessin, hier ist dein Freund!" en="Princess, your friend is here!" />
</Dialogue>

She opened the door, saw the frog, slammed it shut and sat down again, pale.

<Dialogue title="The king" setting="The great hall">
  <Line who="King" de="Wer ist das?" en="Who is it?" />
  <Line who="Princess" de="Ein Frosch. Er will mein Freund sein." en="A frog. He wants to be my friend." />
</Dialogue>

She had to tell him the whole story. The king said that what you have promised, you must
keep.

<Dialogue title="A promise is a promise" setting="The great hall">
  <Line who="King" de="Dann ist er dein Freund. Er isst hier, und er trinkt hier." en="Then he's your friend. He eats here, and he drinks here." />
</Dialogue>

So the frog sat on her chair, then on the table, and ate from her golden plate. She could
hardly swallow a bite. When he had eaten his fill, he had one more wish.

<Dialogue title="Bedtime" setting="The great hall, late">
  <Line who="Frog" de="Ich bin müde. Wo ist das Bett?" en="I'm tired. Where's the bed?" />
</Dialogue>

## The wall

The princess began to cry. She carried him upstairs between two fingers and put him in a
corner. But he crept over to her and said he was tired and wanted to sleep, just like her, and if
she didn't lift him up, he'd tell her father. Then she was furious. She picked him up and threw him
against the wall with all her strength.

When he fell, he was no frog any more.

<Dialogue title="Not a frog" setting="The princess's room">
  <Line who="Prince" de="Prinzessin! Ich bin ein Prinz!" en="Princess! I'm a prince!" />
</Dialogue>

A wicked witch had cast a spell on him, he told her, and only she could have freed him.
The next morning a carriage came for them, drawn by eight white horses, and on the back
stood his faithful servant Heinrich. Heinrich had been so sad when his master became a
frog that he'd had three iron bands put round his heart, to stop it bursting. On the way
home, one after another, the bands burst with a crack, because his master was free and
happy.

## The real story

**Hear the rules you know.** <Say de="Ich will dein Freund sein." />: *will* second,
*sein* at the very end (lesson 12). And <Say de="Ich kann nicht laufen!" /> is the
Grimms' own frog, almost word for word: *ich kann nicht so laufen wie du*, "I can't keep up
with you".

**The king's line is a modal too.** In the Grimms' German he says *Was du versprochen hast,
das musst du auch halten*: what you have promised, you must also keep.

**No kiss.** In the Grimms' tale the princess doesn't kiss the frog; she throws him at the
wall. The kiss came later, in retellings.

**Number one.** *Der Froschkönig oder der eiserne Heinrich*, "The Frog King, or Iron
Henry", opens the Grimms' collection, first printed in 1812. The lines quoted here are from their
last edition, of 1857.
