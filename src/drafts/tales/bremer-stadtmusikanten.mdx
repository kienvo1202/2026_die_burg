<Initial>

Four animals, all of them old, all of them about to be done away with by the people they
had served, and all of them on the run. They set off for Bremen to become town musicians, and never get there. The Brothers Grimm
collected this story in the early 1800s. Listen for the words from the bakery, and for
*der* and *die*: you can now hear which animal is which kind of word.

</Initial>

## The donkey and the dog

A man had a donkey who had carried sacks to the mill for years. Now the donkey was old
and weak, and his master wanted to be rid of him. The donkey saw what was coming and
ran away, towards Bremen. In Bremen, he thought, a donkey with a good loud voice could
become a town musician.

On the road he found a dog lying by the wayside, panting.

<Dialogue title="On the road" setting="A country road, mid-morning">
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Hallo! Wie heißt du?" en="Hello! What's your name?" />
  <Line who="Dog" de="Ich heiße Packan. Ich bin alt. Ich bin zwölf." en="My name is Packan. I'm old. I'm twelve." />
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Ich auch! Ich bin alt!" en="Me too! I'm old!" />
  <Line who="Dog" de="Und jetzt?" en="And now what?" />
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Bremen! Musik!" en="Bremen! Music!" />
</Dialogue>

*Packan* is a good name for a hunting dog: "grab-it". He was too old to hunt, and his
master had wanted to kill him. He went along.

## The cat and the rooster

Soon they found a cat sitting by the road with a face like three days of rain. Her teeth
were blunt, she would rather sit by the stove than chase mice, and her mistress wanted to
drown her.

<Dialogue title="A long face" setting="Further down the road">
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Hallo, Bartputzer!" en="Hello, Whisker-wiper!" />
  <Line who="Cat" de="Hallo. Ich bin alt. Ich bin fünfzehn." en="Hello. I'm old. I'm fifteen." />
  <Line who="Donkey and dog" de="Ich auch! Ich auch!" en="Me too! Me too!" />
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Bremen! Musik! Ja oder nein?" en="Bremen! Music! Yes or no?" />
  <Line who="Cat" de="Ja!" en="Yes!" />
</Dialogue>

Then they passed a farmyard where a rooster was crowing for all he was worth. Guests were
coming on Sunday, and the farmer's wife had told the cook to put him in the soup.

<Dialogue title="The farmyard gate" setting="A farm, early afternoon">
  <Line who="Rooster" de="Kikeriki! Kikeriki!" en="Cock-a-doodle-doo!" />
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Hallo, Rotkopf! Bremen? Musik?" en="Hello, Redhead! Bremen? Music?" />
  <Line who="Rooster" de="Wie bitte?" en="Sorry?" />
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Bremen! Ja oder nein?" en="Bremen! Yes or no?" />
  <Line who="Rooster" de="Ja! Ja! Kikeriki!" en="Yes! Yes! Cock-a-doodle-doo!" />
</Dialogue>

The donkey's words to the rooster are the most famous line in the tale:

<Chunk
  de="Etwas Besseres als den Tod findest du überall."
  literal="Something better than the death you-find everywhere."
  means="You'll find something better than death anywhere."
>

You don't need these words yet. Germans quote this line when someone is stuck somewhere
miserable: anything is better than staying.

</Chunk>

## The robbers' house

Bremen was too far to reach in a day. At nightfall the four of them stopped in a forest,
and the rooster, from the top of a tree, saw a light. It was a house, and through the
window the donkey, the tallest, could see a table covered in food, and robbers eating it.

<Dialogue title="At the window" setting="A house in the forest, at night">
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Brot! Kuchen!" en="Bread! Cake!" />
  <Line who="Dog" de="Lecker!" en="Delicious!" />
</Dialogue>

So they made music. The donkey put his front feet on the window sill, the dog jumped on
his back, the cat climbed onto the dog and the rooster flew up and sat on the cat's head.
Then all four sang at once:

<Dialogue title="The concert" setting="Outside the window">
  <Line who="Donkey" de="I-A!" en="Hee-haw!" />
  <Line who="Dog" de="Wau, wau!" en="Woof, woof!" />
  <Line who="Cat" de="Miau!" en="Meow!" />
  <Line who="Rooster" de="Kikeriki!" en="Cock-a-doodle-doo!" />
</Dialogue>

They crashed through the window in a shower of glass. The robbers thought a ghost had
come for them and fled into the forest. The animals ate everything on the table, put out
the light and each found a place to sleep.

Late in the night one robber crept back. In the dark kitchen he took the cat's glowing
eyes for embers and held a match to them. The cat flew at his face, the dog bit his leg,
the donkey kicked him, and the rooster crowed from the rafters. The robber ran all the way
back to his band.

<Dialogue title="Back in the forest" setting="The robbers' camp, after midnight">
  <Line who="Robber" de="Eine Hexe! Eine Hexe!" en="A witch! A witch!" />
</Dialogue>

He told them a witch had scratched him, a man with a knife had stabbed him, a black
monster had clubbed him, and a judge on the roof had shouted for him. The robbers never
went back.

And the four animals? They liked the house so much that they stayed.

<Dialogue title="The next morning" setting="The house in the forest">
  <Line who="Dog" de="Und Bremen?" en="And Bremen?" />
  <Line who="Donkey" de="Nein. Hier!" en="No. Here!" />
</Dialogue>

## The real story

**They never reach Bremen,** but Bremen adopted them anyway. A bronze statue of the four,
standing on each other's backs, stands beside the town hall. People rub the donkey's front
legs for luck, and they shine gold.

**Animals speak German too.** A German donkey says <Say de="I-A" />, a dog
<Say de="Wau, wau" />, a cat <Say de="Miau" /> and a rooster <Say de="Kikeriki" />. Listen
to the rooster and decide whose version is closer.

**Hear the genders.** <Say de="der Esel" />, <Say de="der Hund" />, <Say de="der Hahn" />,
and <Say de="die Katze" />. Three *der* words and one *die* word: the cat is the odd one
out, in the story and in grammar.
