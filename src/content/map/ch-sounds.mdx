German *ch* has two sounds, and the vowel in front of it decides which.

| after | sound | examples |
|---|---|---|
| a, o, u, au | throaty, like Scottish *loch* | <Say de="acht" />, <Say de="auch" />, <Say de="Kuchen" /> |
| everything else | a soft hiss, like the *h* in *huge* | <Say de="ich" />, <Say de="sprechen" />, <Say de="natürlich" /> |

<Say de="Ich auch!" /> has both, the soft one first.

**Still in the fog:** the ending *-chen* that means "little" is always soft, even after
*a, o, u* (you will meet it later in A1). <span lang="de">*Kuchen*</span> isn't one of them.

Also in the fog: *chs* (usually "ks", as in <span lang="de">*sechs*</span>) and words
borrowed from other languages.
