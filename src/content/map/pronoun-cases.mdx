A pronoun for a thing follows its article, and only a *der* word changes when it's the
thing you take, buy or need:

| the thing | doing | taken, bought, needed |
|---|---|---|
| <Say de="der Pullover" /> | <Say de="er" /> | <Say de="ihn" /> |
| <Say de="die Jacke" /> | <Say de="sie" /> | <Say de="sie" /> |
| <Say de="das Hemd" /> | <Say de="es" /> | <Say de="es" /> |
| <Say de="die Schuhe" /> | <Say de="sie" /> | <Say de="sie" /> |

**Remember it by:** the *-n* of *den* comes back in *ihn*: <Say de="den Pullover" /> →
<Say de="ihn" />. <Say de="Der Mantel? Ich nehme ihn." />

**In English:** Old English had *hine* (him, as the one something is done to) beside
*him* (to him). English kept only *him*; German kept both endings, *ihn* and *ihm*.

**Still in the fog:** *mich*, *dich*, *uns*, *euch* (only in phrases so far), and the "to
him" forms *mir*, *dir*, *ihm*, *ihr* (*Das steht dir!* is a phrase until then).
