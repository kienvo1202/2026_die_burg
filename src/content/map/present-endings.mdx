Take the dictionary form, drop the *-en* to find the stem, and add the ending for the
person. The ending tells you who is doing it, even before you see the pronoun.

| | <span lang="de">*wohnen*</span> | <span lang="de">*kommen*</span> | ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ich** | <Say de="ich wohne" /> | <Say de="ich komme" /> | -e |
| **du** | <Say de="du wohnst" /> | <Say de="du kommst" /> | -st |
| **Sie** | <Say de="Sie wohnen" /> | <Say de="Sie kommen" /> | -en |

**Remember it by:** *thou comest* is <Say de="du kommst" />. English had the endings too,
and dropped them.

**The polite gift:** the *Sie* form is the dictionary form, for every verb except
<Say de="sein" />.

**Still in the fog:** the endings for *er, sie, es, wir, ihr* and plural *sie*, later in
A1.1.
