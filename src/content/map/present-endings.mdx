Take the dictionary form, drop the *-en* (or just *-n*: <span lang="de">*wandern*</span>) to find the stem, and add the ending for the
person. The ending tells you who is doing it, even before you see the pronoun.

| | <span lang="de">*wohnen*</span> | <span lang="de">*spielen*</span> | ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ich** | <Say de="wohne" /> | <Say de="spiele" /> | -e |
| **du** | <Say de="wohnst" /> | <Say de="spielst" /> | -st |
| **er · sie · es** | <Say de="wohnt" /> | <Say de="spielt" /> | -t |
| **wir** | <Say de="wohnen" /> | <Say de="spielen" /> | -en |
| **ihr** | <Say de="wohnt" /> | <Say de="spielt" /> | -t |
| **sie · Sie** | <Say de="wohnen" /> | <Say de="spielen" /> | -en |

**Remember it by:** *thou comest* is <Say de="du kommst" />. English had the endings too,
and dropped them.

**The gift:** *wir*, *sie* (they) and *Sie* all use the dictionary form, for every verb except *sein*.

**Watch for:** stems ending in an s-sound (<Say de="du heißt" />, <Say de="du tanzt" />),
and a few dozen verbs that change their vowel for *du* and *er/sie/es*:
<Say de="du sprichst" />, <Say de="er liest" />.

*-d* add an *e* (<span lang="de">*es kostet*</span>, <span lang="de">*du arbeitest*</span>),
later in A1.
