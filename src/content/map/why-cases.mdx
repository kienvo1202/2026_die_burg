English changes a few words when they stop being the one *doing* something and become
the one something is *done to*: **he** sees, you see **him**. German does the same with
its articles, and calls the forms *cases*.

So far you have two:

- the form for the one doing or being: <Say de="Der Käse ist teuer." />
- the form for the thing had, wanted or needed (*accusative*):
  <Say de="Ich kaufe den Käse." />

Because the article often shows the role, German can move words around more freely than
English; you will see how in A2.

**Still in the fog:** the two other cases, for the receiver (*dative*) and the owner
(*genitive*), in A2 and B1.
