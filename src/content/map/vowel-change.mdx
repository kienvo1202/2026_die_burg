Some verbs change their vowel, **only for *du* and *er · sie · es***. The endings stay the
usual ones (though *nehmen* also changes its consonants: *nimmst*). Nearly always the
change is *e → i*, *e → ie* or *a → ä*; rarely *au → äu* (*du läufst*, lesson 12).

| | du | er · sie · es |
|---|---|---|
| <span lang="de">*nehmen*</span> | <Say de="du nimmst" /> | <Say de="er nimmt" /> |
| <span lang="de">*essen*</span> | <Say de="du isst" /> | <Say de="er isst" /> |
| <span lang="de">*sprechen*</span> | <Say de="du sprichst" /> | <Say de="er spricht" /> |
| <span lang="de">*lesen*</span> | <Say de="du liest" /> | <Say de="er liest" /> |
| <span lang="de">*fahren*</span> | <Say de="du fährst" /> | <Say de="er fährt" /> |

**Why only two forms:** long ago their endings had an *i* sound that pulled the vowel
towards it, the same kind of change that gave English *foot → feet* (lesson 9).

**Remember it by:** in these verbs, *ich*, *wir*, *ihr* and *sie* (they, and polite
*Sie*) never change: <Say de="ich nehme" />,
<Say de="wir nehmen" />. The word list shows the
*du* and *er* forms of every verb, so you'll know which ones do.
