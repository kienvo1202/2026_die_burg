The form for the thing you have, want, need or buy. **Only *der* words change**, and
they always end in *-n*:

| | doing / being | had, wanted, needed |
|---|---|---|
| *der* words | <Say de="der Käse" />, <Say de="ein Käse" />, <Say de="kein Käse" /> | <Say de="den Käse" />, <Say de="einen Käse" />, <Say de="keinen Käse" /> |
| *die* words | <Say de="die Milch" /> | the same |
| *das* words | <Say de="das Ei" /> | the same |
| plurals | <Say de="die Eier" /> | the same |

**Remember it by:** *den* is the *him* of the article. English kept the idea in he/him,
she/her, we/us, they/them.

**So you order** <Say de="einen Kaffee" />, but <Say de="eine Brezel" /> and
<Say de="ein Brötchen" /> don't change here.

**Still in the fog:** accusative pronouns (*mich*, *dich*, *ihn*), prepositions that
always take the accusative, and a few *der* nouns that change too
(<span lang="de">*den Herrn*</span>, <span lang="de">*den Namen*</span>).
