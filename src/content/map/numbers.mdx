Over twenty, German says the units first: <Say de="einundzwanzig" /> is
"one-and-twenty", as in *four-and-twenty blackbirds*. Wait for the end of the number.

| | rule | examples |
|---|---|---|
| 0–12 | learn them | <Say de="null" />, <Say de="eins" />, <Say de="zwei" /> … <Say de="zwölf" /> |
| 13–19 | number + *zehn* | <Say de="dreizehn" />, <Say de="neunzehn" /> |
| 40–90 | number + *zig* (20 and 30: <Say de="zwanzig" />, <Say de="dreißig" />) | <Say de="vierzig" />, <Say de="achtzig" /> |
| 21–99 | units + *und* + tens | <Say de="siebenundfünfzig" /> |

**Four quirks:** <Say de="dreißig" /> (ß), <Say de="sechzehn" /> and
<Say de="sechzig" /> (no s), <Say de="siebzehn" /> and <Say de="siebzig" /> (no -en),
<Say de="einundzwanzig" /> (no s before *und*).

**On the phone,** two is often <Say de="zwo" />, so it can't be mistaken for *drei*.

**Prices:** 2,40 € is <Say de="zwei Euro vierzig" />.

**Still in the fog:** numbers above a hundred, and the clock.
