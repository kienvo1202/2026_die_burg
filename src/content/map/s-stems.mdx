<Say de="heißen" /> follows the normal endings, with one exception: the stem already ends
in an *s*-sound, so the *du*-ending is just *-t*. German doesn't stack two *s*-sounds.

| | |
|---|---|
| **ich** | <Say de="ich heiße" /> |
| **du** | <Say de="du heißt" />, not "heißst" |
| **Sie** | <Say de="Sie heißen" /> |

**Remember it by:** Byron's "Childe Harold was he *hight*". *Hight* is the English twin
of *heißen*, and like *heißen* it means "called", which is why Germans ask *how* you are
called:
<Say de="Wie heißt du?" />

**Still in the fog:** other verbs whose stems end in *s, ß, z* or *x* do the same; you will
meet them in later lessons.
