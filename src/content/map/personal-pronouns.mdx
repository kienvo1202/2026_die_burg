| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| I / we | <Say de="ich" /> | <Say de="wir" /> |
| you (friends) | <Say de="du" /> | <Say de="ihr" /> |
| he / she / it · they | <Say de="er" />, <Say de="sie" />, <Say de="es" /> | <Say de="sie" /> |
| you (polite) | <Say de="Sie" /> | <Say de="Sie" /> |

**For things, the pronoun follows the article:** <span lang="de">*der*</span> → *er*,
<span lang="de">*die*</span> → *sie*, <span lang="de">*das*</span> → *es*.
<Say de="Der Schrank ist alt, aber er ist groß." /> The wardrobe is a "he".

**Remember it by:** Old English did the same. The sun was "she" (*sēo sunne*), the moon
"he" (*se mōna*), as in <Say de="die Sonne" /> and <Say de="der Mond" /> today.

**For people,** *er* or *sie* by who they are. (<span lang="de">*Das Mädchen*</span> is
grammatically *es*; in everyday speech people often say *sie*.)

**Three sies:** <span lang="de">*sie*</span> (she), <span lang="de">*sie*</span> (they)
and <span lang="de">*Sie*</span> (polite you). The verb and the capital tell them apart (at the start of a sentence, only the verb and the situation):
<span lang="de">*sie spielt*</span>, <span lang="de">*sie spielen*</span>,
<span lang="de">*Sie spielen*</span>.

**Still in the fog:** <span lang="de">*mich, mir, ihn*</span>: pronouns that change with
their job in the sentence.
