Every German noun is a *der*, *die* or *das* word, and the group belongs to the **word**,
not to the thing: <Say de="das Mädchen" /> (the girl) is a *das* word.

| *der* | *die* | *das* |
|---|---|---|
| <Say de="der Kuchen" /> | <Say de="die Brezel" /> | <Say de="das Brötchen" /> |
| <Say de="der Kaffee" /> | <Say de="die Bäckerei" /> | <Say de="das Brot" /> |

**Remember it by:** Old English had three words for *the* too: *se*, *sēo*, *þæt*. English
merged them, and kept *þæt* as **that**. German <span lang="de">*das*</span> is English
*that*.

**One sure clue:** every noun made small with *-chen* (little) is *das*. (<span lang="de">*der Kuchen*</span> only looks like one.)

**How to learn them:** always with the article, as one unit. In word lists, blue is
*der*, red is *die*, green is *das*.

**Still in the fog:** more endings that give the gender away, later in A1.
