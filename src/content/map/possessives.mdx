*My* and *your* are <Say de="mein" /> and <Say de="dein" />: English's old *mine* and
*thine*. They take the same endings as *ein*.

| | *my* | *your* (friend) | *his* | *her* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *der* words | <Say de="mein Bruder" /> | <Say de="dein Bruder" /> | <Say de="sein Bruder" /> | <Say de="ihr Bruder" /> |
| *die* words | <Say de="meine Schwester" /> | <Say de="deine Schwester" /> | <Say de="seine Schwester" /> | <Say de="ihre Schwester" /> |
| *das* words | <Say de="mein Kind" /> | <Say de="dein Kind" /> | <Say de="sein Kind" /> | <Say de="ihr Kind" /> |
| plurals | <Say de="meine Eltern" /> | <Say de="deine Eltern" /> | <Say de="seine Eltern" /> | <Say de="ihre Eltern" /> |

**The owner picks the word, the thing picks the ending:** Paul and his daughter,
<Say de="seine Tochter" />; Ama and her husband, <Say de="ihr Mann" />.

**Also:** <Say de="unser" /> (our), <Say de="euer" /> (your, friends; *eure* with an
ending), <Say de="Ihr" /> (your, polite): <Say de="Wie ist Ihr Name?" />

**Still in the fog:** possessives for the thing you have or need
(<span lang="de">*meinen Bruder*</span>), which follow lesson 8's accusative.
